# Project Proposal: Does Muon Pretraining Change What LoRA Can Learn?

**Course:** EN.705.743 ChatGPT from Scratch, Fall 2026
**Team:** John Roth (individual)
**Full design document:** `proposal.md` in the project repository (github.com/jroth1414/rank-and-file)

**Idea.** I will replace the optimizer in the GPT training loop with Muon (Liu et al. 2025), which orthogonalizes the update to every two-dimensional weight matrix instead of scaling each entry by its own gradient history as AdamW does, and test whether that change alters what a low-rank adapter (LoRA) can learn afterwards. I will pretrain matched pairs of small (124M-parameter) language models on identical data, in identical order, with identical architecture, one pair member with AdamW and one with Muon, then fine-tune each with full fine-tuning and with LoRA at ranks 4, 16, and 64. The hypothesis is spectral: Muon's orthogonalized updates should leave flatter singular-value spectra in the weights (H1), the full fine-tuning update on a Muon model should have higher effective rank (H2), and a low-rank adapter should therefore recover a smaller fraction of the full fine-tuning improvement on the Muon model, with the gap closing as rank grows (H3). A June 2026 paper showed Muon-trained features transfer better under full fine-tuning; nobody has tested whether that survives the low-rank restriction that almost all practical fine-tuning uses, and that is the gap this project fills. The result may well be negative or reversed, and either outcome is reportable.

**How it differs from the assignment GPT.** The assignment model is a GPT-2-style decoder trained with AdamW. I keep the decoder-only transformer, the byte-level BPE tokenizer, and the AdamW training loop from the assignments as the baseline arm, and change two things. The training loop gains a second optimizer, Muon, implemented from scratch with Newton-Schulz orthogonalization and the weight-decay and update-scaling corrections of Liu et al.; the fine-tuning loop gains a from-scratch LoRA implementation. To keep the comparison fair I also adopt the Qwen3 architecture recipe (RMSNorm pre-norm, SwiGLU, RoPE, grouped-query attention, QK-norm, tied embeddings, no biases) for both arms, because it was developed under AdamW and is therefore neutral to the optimizer; the report will tabulate every difference from the assignment GPT and include a comparison against the provided assignment weights so the link to the course model is explicit.

**Experiments and metrics.** Three pretraining arms: P1 (AdamW, 2.5B tokens), P2 (Muon, 2.5B tokens), and P3 (AdamW continued until it reaches P2's validation loss), so that "Muon trained a better model" can be separated from "Muon trained a different model." Each arm is fine-tuned on two tasks, continued pretraining on 100M tokens of Python (metric: held-out code loss) and a supervised bundle of SST-2, BoolQ, and AG News as label-word prompts (metric: accuracy), with forgetting measured as the rise in pretraining validation loss. The primary metric is the **LoRA gap**, the fraction of the full fine-tuning improvement that LoRA recovers at each rank, plotted against rank per arm and task. Alongside it I compute effective rank and stable rank of every projection matrix, the same for the full fine-tuning weight update, and the top-r energy fraction of that update, and test whether the last predicts the measured LoRA gap. Learning rates are swept once per optimizer and once per fine-tuning method, never per arm. The minimal deliverable is P1 versus P2 at one seed with the LoRA-gap curve and weight spectra; P3, second seeds, and an attention-head analysis are tiered on top and will be cut first if the schedule slips. Everything runs on one RTX 5070 Ti (about 3.5 GPU-hours per billion tokens), roughly two days of pretraining plus one day of fine-tuning.

**Datasets and libraries.** Pretraining: FineWeb-Edu (sample-10BT), pre-tokenized once with a 32k BPE tokenizer trained on the same corpus. Code fine-tuning: codeparrot/codeparrot-clean, permissive licenses only. Supervised fine-tuning: SST-2, BoolQ, AG News via Hugging Face `datasets`. Libraries: PyTorch, `tokenizers`, `datasets`, NumPy, `safetensors`, PyYAML, Matplotlib. No training frameworks and no PEFT library; the model, Muon, LoRA, training and fine-tuning loops, and spectral analysis are all implemented in the repository.
